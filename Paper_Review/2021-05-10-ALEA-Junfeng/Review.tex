In this paper, the author studies the following space-time fractional
stochastic heat equation:
\begin{align*}
	\partial^\beta_t u(t,x) = -\nu (-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}} u(t,x)
	+I_t^{1-\beta}\left(\lambda \sigma(u(t,x)) \dot{F}_\rho(t,x)\right),
	\quad t>0,\: x\in\R,
\end{align*}
where $\beta\in (0,1)$, $\nu>0$, $\alpha\in(1,2]$ and $\lambda>0$. The main
novelty of the paper is the study of of the spatially inhomogeneous white noise
$\dot{F}_\rho$; see \eqref{E:F} below. There are many grammar/spelling issues
in the paper. We list some of those up to page 9. The author are highly
recommended to go over the paper again to check out all similar issues. In
terms of mathematics, the results seem not complete and there are also some
issues in the proofs of the main results; see below. We recommend a {\bf major
revision} of the paper.


Suggestions and concerns:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item The noise is introduced in Definition 1. It would be better to
		explicitly write out
		\begin{align}
			\label{E:F}
			\E\left(\dot{F}_\rho(t,x)\dot{F}_\rho(s,y)\right) =
			\delta_0(t-s)\rho(x-y)\delta_0(x-y).
		\end{align}
	\item At the end of Section 2.1, can you give some interesting, important,
		nontrivial examples of $\rho$? Since this is the main contribution of the
		paper, one wants to see why such extension is necessary and important and
		how different properties it brings.
	\item Theorem 1: It seems that the conditions for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are not
		optimal. The requirement on $\alpha \in (1,2]$ can be relaxed. Indeed, by
		\cite{CHN19}, the solvability is done under the following condition:
		\begin{align*}
			d < 2\alpha + \frac{\alpha}{\beta} \min\left(2 \gamma -1,0\right)
		\end{align*}
		By setting $d=1$ and  $\gamma=1-\beta$ for this paper, we see that
		\begin{align*}
			1<2\alpha + \frac{\alpha}{\beta}\min\left(1-2\beta,0\right),
		\end{align*}
		or equivalently,
		\begin{align*}
			\alpha > \frac{1}{2} \vee \beta.
		\end{align*}
		So it seems the right condition on $\alpha$ should be
		\begin{align*}
			\alpha \in (1/2\vee \beta, 2].
		\end{align*}
		Moreover, I don't see why you cannot let $\beta=1$ in your paper. Can you
		explain this point?
	\item Remark 2, point No. 2: When $\rho\equiv c$, this reduces to the standard
		space-time white noise. All relevant statements in Theorem 1 has been also
		proved in \cite{CHN19}. Please make the comparison to see if the moment
		exponents in Eq. (6) and H\"older exponents you obtained are consistent
		with the results in \cite{CHN19}.
	\item In the third display on p. 13, the author uses
		\begin{align}
			\label{E:DG}
			\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_t(x-y) \le \frac{C}{t} G_t(x-y)
		\end{align}
		to prove the time increments. First of all, I am not totally sure of this
		inequality, when one refers to [1], please give the precise equation or
		theorem so that the reader can easily local this result. Secondly, the
		left-hand side of \eqref{E:DG} is signed while the right-hand side is
		nonnegative. But when applying for this inequality, one needs to take an
		absolute value. Would this cause any issue? In other words, one may want
		the following bounds instead:
		\begin{align*}
			\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_t(x-y) \right|\le \frac{C}{t} G_t(x-y)
		\end{align*}
		This kind of inequality does not seem correct. One may try the standard
		heat kernel $G_t(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} e^{- \frac{x^2}{2t}}$. In this
		case, we see that
		\begin{align*}
			\frac{\partial}{\partial t}	G_t(x) =
			\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x^2}{t}-1\right) \frac{G_t(x)}{t}.
		\end{align*}
		One cannot find a constant $C$ that is uniformly in $t,x$ so that
		\eqref{E:DG} is true. Even this paper handles the case $\beta<1$, I don't
		expect much difference there.
\end{enumerate}
