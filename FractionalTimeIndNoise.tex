\begin{NickQuestion}
In the Wave equation paper you showed that for the Greens function, we have that
\[
	\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot)(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t |\xi|)}{|\xi|}
\]
and so we see that
\[
	\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot)(c\xi) = \frac{1}{c}\mathcal{F}(ct, \cdot)(\xi)
\]

However in the Fractional SPDE paper the greens function is more complicated and we have that

\begin{align*}
	\mathcal{F}Y_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma, d}(t, \cdot)(\xi) &= t^{\beta + \gamma -1} E_{\beta, \beta + \gamma}(-2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |\xi|^\alpha)
	\\&= t^{\beta + \gamma -1} H^{1,1}_{1,2}\left(2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |\xi|^\alpha \bigg\vert \begin{matrix} (0,1) &
	\\ (0,1) & (1- \beta - \gamma ,\beta) \end{matrix} \right)
	\\&= t^{\beta + \gamma -1} \frac{1}{2 \pi i} \int_L \mathcal{H}^{1\;1}_{1\;2}(s) \left(2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |\xi|^\alpha\right)^{-s} \ud s
\end{align*}

And so we see that
\[
\mathcal{F}Y_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma, d}(t, \cdot)(c\xi) =	t^{\beta + \gamma -1} \frac{1}{2 \pi i} \int_L \mathcal{H}^{1\;1}_{1\;2}(s) \left(2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |c\xi|^\alpha\right)^{-s} \ud s
\]

However I am unsure how to handle the constant, $c$, on the inside so we can get a nice scaling property like we do in the Wave Equation paper.

Also, instead of using the Fox-H function we can just say
\begin{align*}
		\mathcal{F}Y_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma, d}(t, \cdot)(\xi) &= t^{\beta + \gamma -1} E_{\beta, \beta + \gamma}(-2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |\xi|^\alpha)
		\\&= t^{\beta + \gamma -1} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \dfrac{\left(-2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |\xi|^\alpha\right)^k}{\Gamma\left(\beta k + (\beta + \gamma)\right)}
\end{align*}
and so
\[
		\mathcal{F}Y_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma, d}(t, \cdot)(c\xi) = t^{\beta + \gamma -1} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \dfrac{\left(-2^{-1}\nu t^\beta |c\xi|^\alpha\right)^k}{\Gamma\left(\beta k + (\beta + \gamma)\right)}
\]
but I still don't see how to handle the $c$.
\end{NickQuestion}

\begin{NickQuestion}
	In the wave equation paper with Xia Chen you said that that fundamental solution to the SPDE
\begin{equation}
    \label{wspde}
    \begin{cases}
	\dfrac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x) = \sqrt{\Theta}u(t,x)\dot W(x) & \text{$x\in \R^d$ , $t>0$} \\
	u(0,\cdot) = 1                                                                                  &                            \\
	u_t(0,\cdot) = 0
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the mild solution is defined to be
\begin{equation} \label{weqs}
u(t,x) = 1 + \sqrt{\Theta}\int_0^t \left( \int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-y)u(s,y) W(\delta y \right) \ud s
\end{equation}
but for general initial conditions for the wave equation we have the solution to be

\begin{align}
    \label{gweqs}
    u(t,x) = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-y)u(0,y) \ud y &+ \int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-y)u_t(0,y) \ud y \nonumber \\
									    & + \sqrt{\Theta}\int_0^t \left( \int_{\R^d}G(t-s,x-y)u(s,y) W(\delta y) \right) \ud s
\end{align}

But when the initial conditions are such as in \ref{wspde} then the first two integral in \ref{gweqs} are $1$ and $0$ respectively and so we arrive at \ref{weqs}.

In the fractional SPDE
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
    \left(\partial^\beta + \frac{\nu}{2}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\right)u(t,x) = I^\gamma_t \left[\rho(u(t,x))\dot W(x) \right] & \text{$x\in \R^d$ , $t>0$} \\
    u(0,\cdot) = \mu                                                                                                       & \beta \in (0,1]            \\
    u(0,\cdot) = \mu_0, \quad u_t(0,\cdot) = \mu_1                                                                         & \beta \in (1,2]
\end{cases}  \label{fspde}
\end{equation}
and we define
\[
J_0(t.x) =
\begin{cases}
    (Z(t,\cdot)*\mu)(x)                              & \beta \in (0,1] \\
    (Z^*(t,\cdot)*\mu_0)(x) +  (Z(t,\cdot)*\mu_1)(x) & \beta \in (0,1]
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
I(t,x) \iint_{[0,t] \times \R} Y(t-s, x-y) \rho (u(s,y)) W(\delta y) \ud s
\]
and the general solution for \ref{fspde} is
\begin{align}
u(t,x) = J_0(t,x) + I(t,x) \label{feqs}
\end{align}

My question is that if we consider the following fractional spde
\begin{equation}
    \label{fspde1}
    \begin{cases}
	\left(\partial^\beta + \frac{\nu}{2}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\right)u(t,x) = I^\gamma_t \left[\rho(u(t,x))\dot W(x) \right] & \text{$x\in \R^d$ , $t>0$} \\
	u(0,\cdot) = 1                                                                                                         & \beta \in (0,1]            \\
	u(0,\cdot) = 1, \quad u_t(0,\cdot) = 0                                                                                 & \beta \in (1,2]
    \end{cases}  
\end{equation}
then can we calculate $J_0(t,x)$? More specifically is $J_0(t,x) = 1$ in this
case? The wave equation paper worked out nicely because in that paper
$J_0(t,x) =1$ and so the solution was able to be written as \ref{weqs}. The
$1$ allows us to easily do the Piccard iteration to get equation (2.3) from
the Wave equation paper with Xia Chen.

So if our solution can not be written as
\[
    u(t,x) = 1 + I(t,x)
\]
I am not sure at the moment how we can come up with an expression for the $f_n's$ in the Weiner Expansion like you did in equation (2.3) in the paper with Xia Chen.
\end{NickQuestion}

Hi, Nick, here is the answer:

Notice that, by Theorem 4.1 of my paper,
\begin{align*}
    (Z_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d}(t,\cdot)*1)(x) & = \calF Z_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d}(t,\cdot)(0)  \\
                                              & = t^{\Ceil{\beta}-1} E_{\beta,\Ceil{\beta}}(0) \\
                                              & = \frac{ t^{\Ceil{\beta}-1} }{\Gamma(\Ceil{\beta})}.
\end{align*}
Hence, when $\beta\in(0,1]$, we see that $(Z_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(t,\cdot)*1)(x)=1$.
When $\beta\in (1,2]$, 
\begin{align*}
    (Z_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d}^*(t,\cdot)*1)(x) & = \calF Z_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d}^*(t,\cdot)(0) \\
                                                & = E_{\beta}(0)                     \\
                                                & = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1)} = 1.
\end{align*}
Hence, under the initial conditions given by \eqref{fspde1}, $J_0(t,x)\equiv 1$.

\begin{NickQuestion}[Scaling Property]
	Suppose that $\mu(\ud \xi) = \varphi(\xi)\ud \xi$ and $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi(cx) = c^{-(d-\Delta)}$ for some $\Delta \in (0,d)$ and for any $c>0$. Also recall that 
	\[
		\mathcal{F}Y(ct,\cdot)(\xi) = c^{\beta+\gamma -1}\mathcal{F}Y(t,\cdot)(t^{\beta/\alpha} \xi). 
	\]
	Then 
	\[
		\Norm{\tilde{f_n}(\cdot,0,t)}_{H^{\otimes n}}^2 = t^{[2(\beta + \gamma -1)+2-\beta \Delta / \alpha]n} \Norm{\tilde{f_n}(\cdot,0,1)}_{H^{\otimes n}}^2
	\]
\textbf{Proof:} 
\begin{align*}
\Norm{\tilde{f_n}(\cdot,0,t)}_{H^{\otimes n}}^2 = \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\rho \in \Sigma_n} \int_{\R^{nd}} \mathcal{F}f_n(\cdot,0,t)^2(\vec{\xi}) \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi}
\end{align*}
where $\varphi(\vec{\xi}) = \varphi(\xi_1)\cdots\varphi(\xi_n)$
\begin{align*}
 & \int_{\R^{nd}} \mathcal{F}f_n(\cdot,0,t)^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi} =  \int_{\R^{nd}} \left( \int_{[0,t]^n<} \overline{\mathcal{F}Y(t-t_n,\cdot)(\xi_1+\cdots+ \xi_n)\cdots \mathcal{F}Y(t_2-t_1,\cdot)(\xi_1)} \right)^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi}
 \\&= t^{2n(\beta+\gamma-1)}\int_{\R^{nd}} \left( \int_{[0,t]^n<} \overline{\mathcal{F}Y\left(1-\frac{t_n}{t},\cdot\right)(t^{\beta/\alpha}(\xi_1+\cdots+ \xi_n))\cdots \mathcal{F}Y\left(\frac{t_2}{t}-\frac{t_1}{t},\cdot\right)(t^{\beta/\alpha}\xi_1)} \right)^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi}
  \\&= t^{2n(\beta+\gamma-1)+2n}\int_{\R^{nd}} \left( \int_{[0,1]^n<} \overline{\mathcal{F}Y\left(1-t_n,\cdot\right)(t^{\beta/\alpha}(\xi_1+\cdots+ \xi_n))\cdots \mathcal{F}Y\left(t_2-t_1,\cdot\right)(t^{\beta/\alpha}\xi_1)} \right)^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi}
  \\&= t^{2n(\beta+\gamma-1)+2n}\int_{\R^{nd}} \left( \int_{[0,1]^n<} \overline{\mathcal{F}Y\left(1-t_n,\cdot\right)(\xi_1+\cdots+ \xi_n)\cdots \mathcal{F}Y\left(t_2-t_1,\cdot\right)(\xi_1)} \right)^2 \varphi(t^{-\beta/\alpha}\vec{\xi})t^{-nd\beta/\alpha}\ud \vec{\xi}
  \\& = t^{2n(\beta+\gamma-1)+2n - n\beta \Delta / \alpha}\int_{\R^{nd}} \left( \int_{[0,1]^n<} \overline{\mathcal{F}Y\left(1-t_n,\cdot\right)(\xi_1+\cdots+ \xi_n)\cdots \mathcal{F}Y\left(t_2-t_1,\cdot\right)(\xi_1)} \right)^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi}
  \\& = t^{2n(\beta+\gamma-1)+2n - n\beta \Delta / \alpha}\int_{\R^{nd}} \mathcal{F}f_n(\cdot,0,1)(\vec{\xi})^2 \varphi(\vec{\xi})\ud \vec{\xi} 
  \\&=  t^{[2(\beta+\gamma-1)+2 - \beta \Delta / \alpha]n}\Norm{\tilde{f_n}(\cdot,0,1)}_{H^{\otimes n}}^2
\end{align*}
\end{NickQuestion}
\begin{NickQuestion}[Guess for the constant (1.20) from the Wave Equation Paper]
	In the wave equation paper, the constant (1.20) was 
\[	
C =	\begin{cases}
		\dfrac{4-\alpha}{3-\alpha} & \text{under Assumption A}
		\\ \\ \dfrac{4-d}{3-d} & \text{under Assumption B or C}
	\end{cases}
\]
I believe in the fractional paper the constant will be 
\[
	\begin{cases}
		\dfrac{2(\beta+\gamma-1)+2 - \beta \Delta / \alpha}{2(\beta+\gamma-1)+1 - \beta \Delta / \alpha} & \text{under Assumption A}
		\\ \\ \dfrac{2(\beta+\gamma-1)+2 - \beta d/ \alpha}{2(\beta+\gamma-1)+1 - \beta d/ \alpha} & \text{under Assumption B or C}
	\end{cases}
\]
I think this because in the wave equation paper where we use $(4-\Delta)$ this gets replaced by $(2(\beta+\gamma-1)+2 - \beta \Delta / \alpha)$ and thus we can guess that 
\[
	\begin{cases}
		4- \Delta \quad  \to \quad  2(\beta+\gamma-1)+2 - \beta \Delta / \alpha
		\\ 3 - \Delta \quad  \to \quad 2(\beta+\gamma-1)+1 - \beta \Delta / \alpha
	\end{cases}
\]
Note that when $\alpha = \beta =2 $ and when $\gamma = 0$ then the guess reduces to the wave equation. 
\end{NickQuestion}
