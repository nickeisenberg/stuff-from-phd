\noindent{Dear Editor},

\vspace{1em}

In this paper we investigate the conditions required to guarantee the existence of an invariant
measure to the stochastic heat equation on the whole space $\R^d$ with an unbounded Lipschitz
diffusion term $b$. To be more precise, we study
\begin{align*}
  \partial_tu(t,x) - \frac{1}{2} \Delta u(t,x) = b(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x),
\end{align*}
with the initial condition $u(0, \cdot) = \mu(\cdot)$, where $\dot{W}$ is the centered Gaussian
noise that is white in time and colored in space. The major challenge to the problem is finding
conditions which guarantee that the probability moments of the solution in a weighted $L^2(\R^d)$
space are bounded in time, which is often the case when some additional drift terms to the equation
are imposed to help control the usual exponential growth of the moments in
time~\cite{misiats.stanzhytskyi.ea:16:existence, misiats.stanzhytskyi.ea:20:invariant,
assing.manthey:03:invariant}. We do not have such a drift term and the literature for such a set up
is far more scarce than when considering the added drift term. An immediate consequence of no drift
term in our equation is that the spatial dimension $d$ has to be at least $3$, i.e., $d\ge 3$.

\vspace{.5em}

Our paper is mostly inspired by Tessitore and Zabczyk's work~\cite{tessitore.zabczyk:98:invariant},
where they prove existence of an invariant measure under the same setting as ours, i.e., on the
whole space and with an unbounded and Lipschitz diffusion term. \textit{The first contribution} of
our paper is more general initial conditions, including the important Dirac delta initial data. Note
that in~\cite{tessitore.zabczyk:98:invariant}, only the constant initial condition is considered,
even though their techniques essentially allow for all bounded functions. We emphasize that the
Dirac delta measure plays an important role in the asymptotic analysis for stochastic heat equation;
see, e.g., the work of~\cite{amir.corwin.ea:11:probability}. \textit{The second contribution}
is clean conditions on the spectral density of the noise. Indeed, the conditions on the spectral
density ({\it ibid.}) are not easy to verify in practice. We identify the following clean and near
optimal condition:
\begin{align*}
  \int_{\R^d} \frac{\widehat{f}(\ud \xi)}{|\xi|^2 \wedge |\xi|^{2(1-\alpha)}} <\infty,
  \quad \text{for some $\alpha\in (0,1)$,}
\end{align*}
where $f$ is the correlation function of the noise and $\widehat{f}$ is its Fourier transform --- the
so-called spectral density; see Remark~\ref{R:CompactForm} of the manuscript for more details.


\vspace{.5em}

% We expand on this by verifying the existence of an invariant measure for a wider class of initial

Our improvements are due to the fact that we work under the random field framework of
Walsh~\cite{walsh:86:introduction}, while in the past, this problem has usually been studied under
the theory of the stochastic differential equation in the infinite-dimensional setting laid out by
Da Prato and Zabcyzyk~\cite{da-prato.zabczyk:14:stochastic}. While both theories have been shown to
be equivalent~\cite{dalang.quer-sardanyons:11:stochastic}, they both offer their own unique set of
tools to work with. Especially the random field framework provides sharper moment estimates on the
moments of the solution, which is crucial to our analysis.

\vspace{1em}

We hope that the content and contributions of the paper fit well the journal and we look forward to
hearing from you. Thank you very much for handling our paper.

\vspace{0.5cm}
