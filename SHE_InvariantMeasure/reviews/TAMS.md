The authors consider the stochastic heat equation (SHE) on R^d where the driving
noise is white in time and colored in space. The main result of this paper is to
provide some conditions on the initial data and the correlation measure (or the
spectral measure) of the noise that guarantees the existence of an invariant
measure in some weighted L^2 space. According to the authors, there is only one
paper (i.e., the paper by Tessitore and Zabczyk in 1998) that provides some
sufficient conditions on the correlation measure that guarantees the existence
of an invariant measure for the SHE on the whole spatial space. However, I think
that other papers deal with invariant measures when the spatial domain is the
whole space, e.g., the KPZ equation on the real line and stochastic 2d or 3d
Naiver-Stokes equations. Gu and Li 2020 consider SHE on R^d and show the
convergence to the stationary distribution, but their noise is smooth in space. 

Regarding the proof of the main theorem (Theorem 1.4), the authors follow the
strategy given in Tessitore and Zabczyk. The essential thing in the proof is to
bound uniformly in time the moments of the solution in some weighted norm. For
this, the authors used some previous results. More precisely, Reference 10
provided the condition (see (1.10) in the paper) on the correlation measure that
guarantees the uniform bound in time of the moment of the solution at each
spatial point. This condition (1.10) seems easier to be checked than the one
provided by Tessitore and Zabczyk. However, the authors assume one more
condition (1.14) on the spectral measure that does not appear in the paper by
Tessitore and Zabczyk.

The merit of this paper is that the authors found the conditions on the
correlation measure that may be easier to be checked than the condition by
Tessitore and Zabczyk. In addition, the conditions for initial functions in this
paper cover more general functions including the Dirac delta measure at zero.
However, it seems to me that the proofs are fairly standard and they seem to
come from the previous results (e.g., the most important thing, which is the
condition that produces the uniform bound of the moment of the solution in time,
comes from the previous paper), which makes me wonder if this paper meets the
very high standards of originality and novelty of TAMS. Furthermore, this
article only suggests some sufficient conditions on the correlation measure and
the initial function, and I am not so sure if  this paper is strong enough to be
within 10-12% of all submitted papers to TAMS.
