  We thank the anonymous referee for careful reading of the paper, some helpful
  comments, and especially pointing out the reference by Gu and
  Li~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations}. In this revision, we have carefully taken
  into all comments from the report. The list of major changes are as follows:

	\begin{enumerate}
    \item pages 3 -- 4, in the both paragraphs after Eq.~(1.9), we have included
      the reference~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations} and made some comparison with
      our current work. As a short summary, the focus of our paper is on the
      conditions on the correlation function/measure and initial conditions in
      order to guarantee the existence of invariant measures. The focus of the
      paper~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations} is different, and convenient conditions
      were made in~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations} for the correlation function,
      which is assumed to be smooth function with compact support. The initial
      condition in~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations} is essentially constant initial
      data with some perturbation. In contrast, our conditions on both
      correlation function/measure and initial conditions are much weaker; see
      the main theorem -- Theorem 1.5 of the paper.
    \item page 5, Figure 1 has been simplified and updated. We postponed the
      conditions formulated using $\mathcal{H}_\alpha(t)$ back in Section 3.1
      when it is first used.
		\item page 5, Remark 1.2 has been added to comment on the including the
      \textit{strengthened Dalang's condition} in our main theorem.
		\item page 7, Remark 1.6 has been included to comment on our conditions on
      the noise. Both this change and the previous one are related to one
      comment from the referee about our additional assumption, namely, the
      \textit{strengthened Dalang's condition}.
		\item Section 3.2, Figure 2 and Remark 3.5 have been created to help the reader. 
      We have also updated Lemma 3.6 accordingly to reflect the changes in items
      2, 3, and 4 above.
    \item In the Acknowledgment section, we include acknowledgment to Yu Gu
      for the discussions on the reference~\cite{gu.li:20:fluctuations}.
	\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}
  Below is the reviewing report from the anonymous referee:
\end{center}
\begin{quotation}
  \it  

  The authors consider the stochastic heat equation (SHE) on $R^d$ where the
  driving noise is white in time and colored in space. The main result of this
  paper is to provide some conditions on the initial data and the correlation
  measure (or the spectral measure) of the noise that guarantees the existence of
  an invariant measure in some weighted $L^2$ space. According to the authors,
  there is only one paper (i.e., the paper by Tessitore and Zabczyk in 1998) that
  provides some sufficient conditions on the correlation measure that guarantees
  the existence of an invariant measure for the SHE on the whole spatial space.
  However, I think that other papers deal with invariant measures when the spatial
  domain is the whole space, e.g., the KPZ equation on the real line and
  stochastic 2d or 3d Naiver-Stokes equations. Gu and Li 2020 consider SHE on
  $R^d$ and show the convergence to the stationary distribution, but their noise
  is smooth in space. 

  Regarding the proof of the main theorem (Theorem 1.4), the authors follow the
  strategy given in Tessitore and Zabczyk. The essential thing in the proof is to
  bound uniformly in time the moments of the solution in some weighted norm. For
  this, the authors used some previous results. More precisely, Reference 10
  provided the condition (see (1.10) in the paper) on the correlation measure that
  guarantees the uniform bound in time of the moment of the solution at each
  spatial point. This condition (1.10) seems easier to be checked than the one
  provided by Tessitore and Zabczyk. However, the authors assume one more
  condition (1.14) on the spectral measure that does not appear in the paper by
  Tessitore and Zabczyk.

  The merit of this paper is that the authors found the conditions on the
  correlation measure that may be easier to be checked than the condition by
  Tessitore and Zabczyk. In addition, the conditions for initial functions in this
  paper cover more general functions including the Dirac delta measure at zero.
  However, it seems to me that the proofs are fairly standard and they seem to
  come from the previous results (e.g., the most important thing, which is the
  condition that produces the uniform bound of the moment of the solution in time,
  comes from the previous paper), which makes me wonder if this paper meets the
  very high standards of originality and novelty of TAMS. Furthermore, this
  article only suggests some sufficient conditions on the correlation measure and
  the initial function, and I am not so sure if  this paper is strong enough to be
  within 10-12\% of all submitted papers to TAMS.

\end{quotation}
